# New roster review after round three

This review reuses the already-vetted Pixel Art Fundamentals criteria in `skill-vetting.md`: silhouette and proportion first, value grouping second, selective detail last. The judgment below is based on the native caster, scout and heavy pose proofs against the preserved round-three roster. The game capture check follows separately.

## Overall judgment

These are genuinely different characters. New head shapes, body proportions, equipment arrangements, clothing and palette families separate all eight from round three. The three heavy classes now have distinct masses, and the caster roster is no longer a variation of the same upright staff carrier. The essential hair, crow and crossbow are retained.

The new direction is materially stronger than another torso-shading pass. It is not uniformly finished: Shadow's initial tail was too geometric, the crossbow's pale rail dominated its bow limbs, and Berserker's narrow neck undercut his broad chest. Druid's softened hood initially made him feel friendlier than the requested tone. Those are focused art fixes, rather than reasons to restart the roster.

## One priority per class

- **Ranger:** The large copper ponytail, open pointed-ear profile and angled recurve immediately distinguish her. Hair is plainly visible. No major native correction requested; check the bow grip and string while mirrored in-game.
- **Sorcerer:** The strongest new caster design. The white swept hair, large shoulder and blue robe establish a new figure. No major native correction requested; check that the cyan spell and pale collar remain separate in game lighting.
- **Shadow:** Initially the trailing lower body looked like a flat triangular kite. The proposed fix is one bend in the contour and a broad shaded fold, retaining its floating gap. The updated fold proof is better; no more texture is needed.
- **Necromancer:** The stooped burgundy occultist is a new silhouette, and the crow has a readable body, beak and tail. No major native correction requested; confirm the dark bird survives crypt lighting.
- **Paladin:** Gold slab armor, a closed helm, halo and two-handed hammer clearly replace the previous winged sword-and-shield figure. The large planes read well. No major native correction requested; inspect hand-to-hammer continuity through the swing.
- **Druid:** Initially reads as a friendly rounded green wizard. Recess the face into the hood and break one large hood/shoulder/hem edge into a rough asymmetric contour. This should make the hermit feel weathered without covering him in noisy bark texture.
- **Berserker:** The twin axes and blue warpaint are clear, but the narrow exposed neck and small high head weaken the broad torso. Lower the head roughly one native pixel and widen the trapezius/neck bridge about two pixels. Preserve the new body and axe poses.
- **Witch Hunter:** Hood, mask and split skirt are an unmistakably new outfit. The initial crossbow's long bright rail resembles a blade or rifle at a glance. Darken the rail to wood/bronze, retain one narrow bolt highlight, and extend the visible bow limbs perpendicular to the stock with negative space around them. Preserve both grips.

These are art-direction judgments, not claims that technical validity makes the sprites attractive. Native pose proofs establish the new designs and obvious connections; actual game captures are still required to judge their final size and background contrast.

## Final game render check

Inspected the final native roster and 96 actual-game frame samples from take `20261030130155`: eight heroes, daylight and crypt settings, with idle, right-facing movement, left-facing movement, both attack directions and moving attacks represented. The compact evidence sheets are `round4-final-review-day.png` and `round4-final-review-crypt.png`. These are sampled render checks, not a claim that I watched every generated animation frame.

The new identities survive at gameplay size. Ranger's copper hair and pointed profile remain visible in both directions. Witch Hunter's enlarged bow limbs and darker rail now read as a crossbow, with the stock held across the body. Necromancer's crow retains its projecting head/beak and wing/tail silhouette against both backgrounds. Shadow retains a clear ground gap, and its folded tail looks less like a flat triangle. Sorcerer's white hair/collar and blue robe separate cleanly; Paladin's halo, helm and hammer remain readable; Druid's face is more recessed; Berserker's head is seated more convincingly on the shoulders.

I found no obvious cropped hats, detached limbs, broken grips or missing signature props in these samples. Combat effects and nearby enemies temporarily cover parts of the characters, as they do in normal gameplay. This does not hide a persistent sprite defect in the inspected samples. The new designs are ready to present as proposals; subjective preference remains for the user to decide.
