# Round 2 independent graphics review

Final independent visual review, updated 15 November 2026. **The revised Ranger, Berserker, Ea-nasir and six boss models pass this graphics review.** No remaining blocking aesthetic, anatomy, equipment-attachment or animation-transition defect was found in the inspected final coverage. This is a graphics verdict, not a claim that the separately reviewed gameplay/browser has no errors. The first review's overly permissive treatment of aesthetic quality is superseded: working frames and attached equipment are necessary but do not make an attractive character.

## Ea-nasir: interim blocker resolved

The interim review rejected Ea-nasir's broad flat face, triangular olive beard, block-like arms, evenly striped skirt and tiny feet. The first review's description of this as a deliberately simpler style was too forgiving. The final replacement resolves that finding with a coherent source model and substantial carved/material depth.

The exact supplied statue photo and final model were opened directly: `tools/eanasir-source/reference/standing-male-worshiper.jpg` and `tools/eanasir-source/model.png`. The distinctive wavy hair, double-arched brow, large bordered eyes, projecting nose, ringed beard and long fringed wrap remain recognizable. The final sculpture-like shoulders, bent forearm, carved beard recesses, diagonal wrap folds and planted feet are far more convincing than the rejected geometric model. The copper disc and whip preserve its game identity. Native S1 necessarily compresses facial detail; the silhouette and warm material separation remain readable, while S2.4 and selection size expose the modeled face and beard.

Final evidence is under `output/hero-round2-2026-11-14/eanasir-art/`. Every idle/gait/attack pose, the loop return and all 80 attack/gait combinations were inspected. Long cloth deliberately hides the upper legs; the hem shifts and the feet alternate beneath it without replacing the statue's upright identity. Actual Game field/crypt captures cover both facings, diagonal travel and stationary/moving attacks. The six shared-boss scenes reach left/right whip, magic and copper releases. The final single whip strand begins at the active hand; the obsolete rear coil does not remain as a competing lash during release. The final 640×420 and short 480×270 selection screens were inspected with the correct model and no showcase clipping.

## Ranger and Berserker

The source-detail revisions are visibly better than the rejected first pass. Ranger restores the original three-quarter cheek/chin, pointed ear, swept hair and cloak/bow silhouette. Its face is no longer a flat enlarged cluster. Berserker restores a broad jaw, thick neck, shaped chest, diagonal harness and substantial shoulders; the first-pass wedge chest and pasted pointed beard are gone. At S2.4 those material and facial distinctions survive in the real scene and selection stage. S1 remains a tiny face and should not be presented as portrait-level detail.

The full chronological idle, walk and attack contacts were inspected, including the return to the first pose. All 80 Ranger and 128 Berserker attack/gait composites were inspected on the refreshed matrices. The deepest passing poses and extended stride poses keep the lower body attached; bow/axe grips and face registration remain coherent. Their existing motion still has stylized arcade proportions and relatively restrained torso rotation. These are improvements toward the reference quality, not proof that they surpass every Heroes reference.

The visual references were opened directly: `example sprites folder/inspection/heroes3/heroes3-grand-elf-contact.png` and `example sprites folder/inspection/heroes2-archer-frames.png`. These show the relevant standard: a consistent character model, recognizable materials, connected limbs and deliberate preparation/release/recovery. They were used as a quality benchmark rather than a demand to copy their costumes.

## Monsters

The source audit covers all 76 enemy/prop archetypes: seven roster pages and the complete multi-frame Skeleton Archer, Skeleton Mage, Bone King and Demon Lord contacts, followed by all final Ea-nasir and replacement-boss poses. This includes every forest, desert, frozen, meadow and hell creature family, the four props and all eight night bosses. The interim inventory of 249 frames is historical and was superseded by the new models; the six replacements alone contain 532 source frames.

The small two-frame creatures use deliberately compact arcade forms: bats/owls/vultures/bee have clear wing silhouettes; spiders/scorpions/mantis keep recognizable limbs; hounds and rodents preserve their horizontal body mass; slimes and oozes use coherent squash. Their simple forms are proportionate to their small gameplay role. Armored knights, casters and larger brutes preserve their identifying headgear/weapons. Bone King is more graphic and flat than the richer Demon Lord, but has a coherent armored silhouette and explicit attack poses.

The six old enlarged two-frame bosses were rejected at the interim gate. Their final replacements now pass:

| Boss | Final visual judgment |
|---|---|
| Butcher | Hooded head, scarred flesh, apron folds, hook and cleaver form a threatening heavy silhouette. Charge preparation lowers the cleaver and leans the body; slam and recovery retain the hand/weapon connection. |
| Lich | Crown, skull, layered purple/gold robes and skull staff read as a powerful caster. Casting arm and robe remain connected through volley, summon and blink; the full-world frame retains its crown and staff. |
| Pharaoh | Gold/lapis headdress, bandaged limbs, weighted robes and complete crook remain consistent through travel and spells. The added six-frame volley preparation joins release and recovery cleanly in both facings. |
| Frost Giant | Horned helm, beard, fur, blue body and large axe have clear separate materials and weight. Both feet remain connected through charge/dash; the long shaft stays one piece through slam. |

All 532 source poses were inspected on the complete rig sheets. Actual Game capture metadata visits every source frame: Butcher 88, Lich 84, Pharaoh 94, Frost Giant 88, Thorn Queen 84, Pit Lord 94. Each 30-second tour includes both facings and relevant attacks. The original Pharaoh/Pit Lord GIFs predated six added windup poses; this reviewer closed that precise gap with four current-Game 1.1-second, 60-fps clips, covering both facings, all six wind frames 88–93, release at frame 70 and recovery. Visual source inspection and chronological runtime transition strips were assessed together; a frame-count assertion alone was not considered aesthetic evidence.

The final shared flash/squash/body repair was also inspected in the playthrough review's `boss-body-stretch.png`: six models, normal/flash/squash/combined transforms, both facings. The drawn silhouette and body bounds remain aligned. Some enlarged runtime contact crops trim the top of tall crowns/staffs; the full-world Lich and Thorn Queen frames were checked separately and are not clipped in Game.

The remaining quality limitation is motion depth: the new bosses are coherent textured rigs derived from one model, with a largely fixed three-quarter torso orientation. Their shoulders, arms, weapons, feet and cloth move, but they do not provide the full torso rotation and foreshortening of independently repainted cel animation. This limits how cinematic a large swing can feel; it does not recreate the disconnected, flat first-pass models. The smaller unchanged monsters and Bone King's more graphic rendering also retain their established simpler style. This review approves the revised art within that stated scope, not a claim that every asset universally surpasses Heroes II/III.

All authored source states and the supplied Game scenarios were reviewed; every possible biome lighting/status-effect combination and every brief attack/gait combination in natural play were not exhaustively captured. Browser functional verification is owned by root; no independent error-free-browser claim is made here. No saved profile was modified. No implementation file was changed by this reviewer.
